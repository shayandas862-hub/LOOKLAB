Five films in two days. Every frame made in code. Sound on.

My last post was film 1: 15 seconds of motion graphics about Look Lab, one of our projects. Since then the films have grown up. Film 2 moved into 3D. Film 3 told a 40-second story with two characters across 13 shots. Film 4 ran 1:32 through eight worlds, and ends on one continuous camera move: out of a screen, round the runner, and up over a ring of worlds.

Film 5 is the one that matters, because it shows the approach.

It starts with one sentence: "a launch film for a running shoe. warm. premium."

The sentence is planned before anything is made. It becomes shot cards, each with its own lens. For each shot, the agent decides how that shot should be made. The shots are built, checked against the brief before anyone sees them, then cut to the music.

The check is the step most tools skip. In the film, shot 1 is painted the wrong orange on purpose. The checker measures it against the brand colour: 5.3, where a pass is under 2.0. It's repainted and measured again: 0.1. The mistake was planted. The measurement is real, taken from the rendered pixels.

The build so far, measured:
Film 1: 15 s, 900 frames, no automatic checks.
Film 3: 40 s, 960 frames. A motion check caught 2 problems.
Film 4: 1:32, 2,208 frames. Its checks caught 11.
Film 5: 1:32, 2,208 frames. Its checks caught 14. I caught none.
Across everything so far: 7,512 frames, 36 problems found by the agent's own checks, 3 found by me.

This is still in progress, and these films aren't the finished look. They're the control layer: every move, lens, cut and colour is set and measured. Generative video gives you realism and makes you fight for control. We're building it the other way round. Next, footage that looks real goes on top of these films, one layer at a time, and each layer is checked against the one underneath so the control isn't lost.

The first realism test is in. We score how closely a generated take keeps the shoe's planned shape, where 1.0 is exact and the plan itself scores 0.995. Take 1 held the shape at 0.90 but added no real surface. Take 2 added a real surface and dropped to 0.57. Not solved yet. That's the next post.

If you make films, ads or brand content and spend more time switching tools than making the thing, follow along.

You write the brief. It hands back a checked film.
