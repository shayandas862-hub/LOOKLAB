## 1. Exactness: products, logos, on-screen text, brand colours

### Takeaway
No source shows any leading video model reliably reproducing a real logo, label text or brand colour. The research and practitioner evidence points the other way. The common professional workaround keeps the real product, logo and text out of the generator and composites them in post, and the best-known AI ads show that pattern.

### Cited Findings
- T2VTextBench (arXiv, submitted 8 May 2025) bills itself as "the first human-evaluation benchmark dedicated to evaluating on-screen text fidelity and temporal consistency in text-to-video models". It tested "ten state-of-the-art systems, ranging from open-source solutions to commercial offerings" and found "most struggle to generate legible, consistent text" — [arXiv 2505.04946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04946) (read 2026-10-15; [paper]; it predates Veo 3/3.1, Kling 3/4 and Seedance 2, so it may overstate today's failure rate).
- "Do generative video models understand physical principles?" (Google DeepMind authors, 14 Jan 2025) found physical understanding "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism" — [arXiv 2501.09038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09038) (read 2026-10-15; [paper]).
- Practitioner and vendor guides converge on compositing. "The product is never AI-generated. It's the real product photo, composited into an AI-generated scene." Another guide says "render the scene without text and composite the real logo, captions, and CTA over the footage in post". A production walkthrough lists "Replace product shots with real renders or photography, fix hands, logos and on-screen text, and stabilize faces across cuts." Flexible packaging and wrap-around labels are named as the hardest case — [primores.org](https://primores.org/wiki/marketing/ai-product-video-fidelity/), [hermoso.ai](https://hermoso.ai/blog/why-ai-ads-look-fake/), [xmamedia.ai](https://www.xmamedia.ai/blog/ai-commercial-examples), [domoai.app](https://domoai.app/blog/ai-product-videos-without-warping-the-product) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary] of vendor and practitioner blogs; these are AI-video sellers' guides, not tested studies).
- One guide gives this mechanism: "AI video models work by redistributing pixels across frames, not by understanding what a logo means. Text and letterforms are precise — a small shift reads as distortion." — [domoai.app](https://domoai.app/blog/ai-product-videos-without-warping-the-product) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary], vendor blog).
- One guide sorts label elements by how exact they must be. P0 is "must be exact: brand mark, product name, dosage, quantity, certification, regulated claim", and P1 is "must remain visually faithful". The same summary says no source measured whether the composite workaround survives audience or regulator scrutiny — [primores.org](https://primores.org/wiki/marketing/ai-product-video-fidelity/) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]).
- Coca-Cola, 2025 "Holidays Are Coming" (released 3 Nov 2025, made by Silverside and Secret Level). On 7 Nov 2025 a widely shared X post showed the Coca-Cola logo being *added* to a frame in Secret Level's behind-the-scenes video ("THAT'S NOT HOW THAT WORKS", about 62,000 likes in two days). Secret Level's behind-the-scenes post on X was deleted around 9–10 Nov 2025 — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/ai-generated-coca-cola-christmas-ad) (read 2026-10-15; [user posts] via [secondary]).
- Toys"R"Us (2024, made with Sora). Ars Technica pointed to "unnatural movement, strange visual artifacts, and the irregular shape of eyeglasses" — [Wikipedia: Toys "R" Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toys_%22R%22_Us) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary] citing [press]; before the April 2025 window).
- Getty v Stability (UK High Court, 4 Nov 2025): Getty won only on "isolated, historic reproductions of ISTOCK and (for v2.1) GETTY IMAGES watermarks". So a model reproducing a third party's mark in output has already produced a trade-mark finding — [AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/getty-images-v-stability-ai-uk-high-court-finds-no-secondary-copyright-infringement-in-landmark-genai-ruling/) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary] of a legal-association note).

### Inferences
- "Exact" (pixel-true logo geometry, legal copy, a specified hex or Pantone colour, the real SKU) and "plausible" (looks like a bottle with a red label) are different targets. Every piece of evidence found shows professionals reaching exactness by compositing real assets or rendering them deterministically, not by prompting. The client's premise is consistent with the evidence.
- Because the Coca-Cola logo was composited in post, even a top-tier brand with two specialist AI studios did not trust the generator with its own logo in 2025.

### Gaps
- I found no benchmark, measured study or vendor claim on **brand-colour accuracy** (ΔE against a target) in generated video. This is untested in public as far as I could reach.
- No 2026 re-run of a text-fidelity benchmark on Veo 3.1, Kling 3/4 or Seedance 2/2.5 was reached (search budget). Text rendering may have improved since May 2025. This needs checking.
- Puma's AI ads and their makers' statements were not reached.

## 2. Continuity between shots (characters, wardrobe, props, lighting, geography)

### Takeaway
Reference features have grown quickly, from 3 reference images (Veo 3.1, Oct 2025) to 12 files (Seedance 2.0, Feb 2026) to a reported 50 (Seedance 2.5, Jul 2026). Vendors still admit object-permanence failures, and the craft workarounds (avoid human close-ups, stabilise faces across cuts) show continuity is not solved. Direct practitioner measurements of multi-shot consistency were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Runway's own Gen-4.5 release (1 Dec 2025) lists "Object permanence: objects may disappear or appear unexpectedly across frames (e.g., a cup vanishing after being occluded)" — [Runway research](https://runway.com/research/introducing-runway-gen-4.5) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]).
- Veo 3.1 in the Gemini API takes "Up to three images to be used as style and content references", and only on 8-second clips. Veo 3.1 Lite does not support reference images — [Gemini API Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]).
- Google's "Ingredients to Video" (15 Oct 2025): "you can use multiple reference images to control the characters, objects and style" — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/ai/veo-updates-flow/) (read 2026-10-15; [press release]).
- Seedance 2.0 (12 Feb 2026) takes up to 12 reference files (9 images, 3 videos, 3 audio) — [letsdatascience.com](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/bytedances-seedance-2.0-spooked-all-of-hollywood-in-72-hours) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary] citing the ByteDance Seed launch blog). Seedance 2.5 (31 Jul 2026): "up to 30 images, 10 video clips, and 10 audio clips as references" — [Wikipedia: Seedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seedance) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary], unverified against ByteDance).
- Seedance 1.0 (June 2025) already generated "multi-shot clips" — [Wikipedia: Seedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seedance) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]).
- Coca-Cola 2025: "This year it avoided close-up AI humans and used computer-generated polar bears, rabbits, and squirrels instead" — [NBC News, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/europe/mcdonalds-ai-generated-christmas-advert-social-media-backlash-rcna248590) (read 2026-10-15; [press]).
- A production walkthrough lists "stabilize faces across cuts" as a post step — [xmamedia.ai](https://www.xmamedia.ai/blog/ai-commercial-examples) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]).

### Inferences
- The number of reference inputs is rising fast, about 3 to 50 in nine months. That is the strongest sign vendors are working on continuity. It is not evidence of success: no source measured identity, wardrobe or prop drift across a real multi-shot sequence.
- Coca-Cola's choice of animals over human close-ups in 2025 reads as a craft workaround for face and identity drift (my inference; NBC reports the choice, not the reason).

### Gaps
- No practitioner numbers (e.g. "N of M shots kept the same face") for Veo 3.1 Ingredients, Kling 3.0 or Seedance 2.x multi-shot were reached. Reddit and X could not be read, and Curious Refuge and befores & afters were not reached.
- Lighting and geography continuity across shots (same light direction, same room layout): I found no source.

## 3. Length: maximum usable clip length and long-form coherence

### Takeaway
Native single-generation length went from 8 s (Veo 3, May 2025) to 15 s (Seedance 2.0, Feb 2026) to a reported 30 s (Seedance 2.5, Jul 2026; Kling 4.0, Oct 2026). Extension features chain clips to minutes. This is the fastest-closing gap. Usable *quality* over long takes is documented only through vendors' own limitation notes and extension constraints.

### Cited Findings
- Veo 3.1 / Veo 3.1 Fast: durations "4, 6, or 8 seconds", 24 fps. Extension adds "7 seconds and up to 20 times", and combined output can reach 148 s. Extension input must be Veo-generated, 141 s or less, and **720p only**. 1080p and 4K are 8-second clips only — [Gemini API Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]).
- Google (15 Oct 2025): Extend "you can create longer videos, even lasting for a minute or more" — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/ai/veo-updates-flow/) (read 2026-10-15; [press release]).
- Veo 3 (May 2025): "Users can create a maximum of eight seconds per clip" — [Wikipedia: Veo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veo_(text-to-video_model)) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]).
- Seedance 2.0 (12 Feb 2026): clips up to 15 s. Seedance 2.5 (31 Jul 2026): up to 30 s, "can be extended over multiple rounds to several minutes" — [Wikipedia: Seedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seedance) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]).
- Kling 4.0 was "officially launched globally in October 2026" with "native 30-second video generation", native 4K and 10-bit HDR. Kling 4.0 Flash was in early access from 28 Sep 2026 — [Wikipedia: Kling AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kling_AI) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary], unverified; Kling's own page not reached).
- Kling 3.0 clips run 3–15 s, billed per second with a 3 s minimum — [evolink.ai](https://evolink.ai/blog/kling-3-api-pricing-integration-guide) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary], reseller).
- Background: OpenAI's Feb 2024 Sora preview promised "up to one minute", and Google's May 2024 Veo announcement claimed 1080p "over a minute long". Shipped single generations in 2025 were 8 s (Veo 3) — [Wikipedia: Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora_(text-to-video_model)), [Wikipedia: Veo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veo_(text-to-video_model)) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]; demo vs. shipped).

### Inferences
- Most commercial shots are a few seconds long, so clip length is close to irrelevant for ads. It matters for long takes, dialogue scenes and long-form work. Extension at 720p only (Veo) means a long Veo take is lower resolution than a single 8 s 1080p/4K clip, so length still trades off against quality.
- Demo claims ran ahead of shipped products by about two years (a 2024 "one minute" demo against 8 s shipped in 2025). An agent's design should rely on shipped specs, not demos.

### Gaps
- I found no measured study of quality decay across extensions (identity drift per extension, artefact rate after N hops).
- Long-form coherence (story-level continuity across minutes): no primary evidence reached beyond vendor limitation notes.

## 4. Control: camera, timing, acting/performance, local edits

### Takeaway
Control is getting more *kinds* (first and last frame, references, object insert and remove, video-to-video edits, timestamp edits). It is still coarse and probabilistic: fixed duration buckets, a fixed frame rate, no numeric camera path in the docs I read, and vendor-admitted failures of cause and effect and of action outcomes, which govern performance.

### Cited Findings
- Veo 3.1 parameters: duration only 4/6/8 s; frame rate 24 fps; "Multi-video prompting is not supported"; "English is fully supported; other languages are untested"; latency from 11 s to 6 minutes at peak; generated videos kept for 2 days — [Gemini API Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]).
- Veo person generation: "In EU, UK, CH, MENA locations, `allow_adult` is the only allowed value for `personGeneration`" — [Gemini API Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]). This is a UK-market constraint (no generated children), India not listed.
- Google, 15 Oct 2025: Frames to Video (first and last frame) and Insert object were announced. Remove object: "Soon, you'll be able to take anything out of a scene". The Gemini API then listed Add and Remove object as "not available at the moment", and audio features as "experimental and actively improving" — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/ai/veo-updates-flow/) (read 2026-10-15; [press release]; current status needs checking).
- Runway Aleph (25 Jul 2025) performs "a wide range of edits on an input video such as adding, removing, and transforming objects", generates "any angle of a scene" and modifies "style and lighting" — [Runway research](https://runway.com/research/introducing-runway-aleph) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]; no limitations stated).
- Runway Gen-4.5 (1 Dec 2025) admitted limitations, quoted exactly. "Causal reasoning: effects sometimes precede causes (e.g., a door opening before the handle is pressed)". "Success bias: actions disproportionately succeed (e.g., a poorly aimed kick still scoring a goal)." Runway framed its control modes (Image to Video, Keyframes, Video to Video) as *to be brought* to Gen-4.5 — [Runway research](https://runway.com/research/introducing-runway-gen-4.5) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]).
- Seedance 2.5 (31 Jul 2026): "Users can edit specific timestamps without regenerating the whole video" — [Wikipedia: Seedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seedance) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]).
- Kling 4.0 (Oct 2026): "expanded keyframe editing support" — [Wikipedia: Kling AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kling_AI) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]).
- Kalshi ad maker PJ Accetturo (June 2025) on prompt batching in Gemini: "I always tell it to return 5 prompts at a time—any more than that and the quality starts to slip" — [CO/AI, 13 Jun 2025](https://getcoai.com/news/kalshis-nba-finals-ad-costs-just-2k-using-googles-ai-video-tool/) (read 2026-10-15; [interview] via [secondary]; CO/AI credits VentureBeat, but its link points elsewhere).
- Seedance Cruise/Pitt clip (Feb 2026): the maker said it was "text-to-video only". An analyst found green-screen stunt footage of the same choreography on Seedance's site, which suggests video-to-video (performance driven by real actors). "This remains unresolved" — [letsdatascience.com](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/bytedances-seedance-2.0-spooked-all-of-hollywood-in-72-hours) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]).

### Inferences
- In the vendor docs I read, camera moves, timing and performance are set by words, reference frames or a driving video, never by numeric parameters (lens mm, dolly speed, a cut on frame N). Frame-accurate timing to music or VO therefore still happens in the edit.
- The "success bias" and "causal reasoning" admissions mean performance beats (a missed catch, a hesitation, a reaction after a cause) are where generation is least trustworthy. The Seedance case hints that the most convincing "generated" action may be driven by real performers.
- "English fully supported; other languages untested" (Veo) matters for Bengali or Hindi prompts and dialogue in the India market.

### Gaps
- No primary doc reached describes numeric camera-path control for Veo, Kling, Seedance or Runway. I did not read every vendor doc, so this needs checking.
- No practitioner measurement of local-edit fidelity (does "change only the jacket" leave every other pixel alone?) was found.

## 5. Cost per usable second: list prices and hit rates

### Takeaway
List prices read today (2026-10-15) run from about $0.02–$0.05/s (lite tiers) to $0.40/s (Veo 3.1 Standard), $0.68/s (Seedance 2.5 1080p) and $1.50/s (Seedance 2.0 4K). The only published hit rate with numbers, the Kalshi ad, is 15 usable clips from 300–400 generations (about 4–5%). That implies a cost per *kept* second roughly 20–27× list price (estimate). Big-brand projects report far larger generation volumes (70,000 clips; "thousands of takes").

### Cited Findings — list prices (read 2026-10-15)
- **Google Gemini API** (page "Last updated 2026-10-15 UTC"), per second with audio: Veo 3.1 Standard $0.40 (720p/1080p), $0.60 (4K). Veo 3.1 Fast $0.10 / $0.12 / $0.30. Veo 3.1 Lite $0.05 / $0.08 (no 4K). No free tier. "charged only if the video is successfully generated" — [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]).
- **Runway API** ($0.01 per credit), per second: gen4.5 $0.12; gen4_turbo $0.05; aleph2 $0.28 (56-credit minimum); act_two $0.05; veo3.1 with audio $0.40, without audio $0.20; veo3.1_fast with audio $0.15; kling3.0_pro with audio $0.17; kling3.0_4k $0.43; seedance2 1080p $0.40, 4K $1.50; seedance2_5 1080p $0.68 (plus 34 credits/s of reference video); wan3 1080p $0.20; hailuo3 768P $0.10; grok_imagine_1_5 1080p $0.29; gemini_omni_flash_1.1 1080p $0.15, 4K $0.30 — [Runway API pricing](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/guides/pricing/) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]). OpenAI's pricing page returned 403.

### Cited Findings — hit rates and volumes
- **Kalshi (NBA Finals ad, June 2025, Veo 3):** 300–400 generations to get 15 usable clips; 2–3 days; about $2,000. "95% cost reduction vs traditional ads" is the maker's claim — [CO/AI, 13 Jun 2025](https://getcoai.com/news/kalshis-nba-finals-ad-costs-just-2k-using-googles-ai-video-tool/) (read 2026-10-15; [interview] via [secondary]). Kalshi confirmed to Business Insider that the ad was made entirely with Veo 3, and its co-founder said a comparable spot would cost seven figures and take months — [Business Insider via search](https://www.businessinsider.nl/the-chaotic-kalshi-ad-during-the-nba-finals-was-made-with-ai-for-2000-the-guy-behind-the-clip-shared-how-he-made-it/) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]; the page redirected and was not read). None of the sources splits the $2,000 between creator fee and generation fees.
- **Coca-Cola 2025:** "Five specialists produced over 70,000 video clips in 30 days, according to the behind-the-scenes voiceover" — [NBC (NBC Miami), 4 Nov 2025](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/national-international/coca-cola-ai-generated-holiday-ad-backlash/3716580/) (read 2026-10-15; [press]).
- **McDonald's Netherlands (Dec 2025):** The Sweetshop's CEO said the team created "thousands of takes" that were shaped in the edit — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/the-most-terrible-ad-of-the-year-mcdonald-s-pulls-ai-ad) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]; the page returned 403).

### Implied cost per usable second (ESTIMATES, my arithmetic, not reported figures)
- Assumptions: Kalshi's hit rate (15 of 300–400 = 3.75–5%) carries over, and every generation is 8 s (Veo 3 / 3.1 maximum). Generated seconds are 2,400–3,200. At most 120 s of kept footage (15 × 8 s).
  - Veo 3.1 Standard ($0.40/s): $960–$1,280 in generation, so **about $64–$85 per kept clip and $8.00–$10.70 per kept second**.
  - Veo 3.1 Fast 1080p ($0.12/s): $288–$384, so **about $19–$26 per kept clip and $2.40–$3.20 per kept second**.
  - Kling 3.0 Pro with audio via Runway ($0.17/s): **about $3.40–$4.55 per kept second**. Seedance 2.0 1080p via Runway ($0.40/s): **about $8.00–$10.70 per kept second**.
- Coca-Cola 2025: 70,000 clips at an assumed 5–8 s each is 350,000–560,000 generated seconds, about **$17,500 (Lite $0.05) to $224,000 (Standard $0.40)** at *today's* Gemini list prices. This is a generation-equivalent figure only: tools, contract prices and final runtime are unknown.
- Caveats: Kalshi was a deliberately chaotic, comedic spot that tolerates oddities. A brand-exact product spot would plausibly keep fewer clips (inference, not measured). Subscription plans (e.g. Flow credits) price differently from the API.

### Inferences
- Generation fees are small next to labour (see §6). The cost that matters is human selection, fixing and editing time multiplied by the low hit rate. An agent that cuts the number of failed generations (better shot planning, references, automatic rejection) attacks the real cost.
- Sora's exit (announced six months after Sora 2 launched) shows that per-second prices and model availability can disappear. An agent tied to one model is exposed.

### Gaps
- No hit rates found for Toys"R"Us, Puma or AI film festival makers (search budget).
- Kling's own price list and OpenAI's (historical) price page were not reachable. Google's Vertex AI per-generation billing was not read.

## 6. Human time still spent fixing output

### Takeaway
The best-documented brand projects still used teams of 5–20+ people for weeks. The McDonald's maker said the hours "far exceeded a traditional shoot". Cost and speed gains are real for some formats (Kalshi: one person, 2–3 days), but "one prompt in, finished video out" is not what professionals report doing.

### Cited Findings
- **McDonald's Netherlands (45 s, Dec 2025):** The Sweetshop's CEO Melanie Bridge wrote "Ten people, five weeks, full-time" and "the hours that went into this job far exceeded a traditional shoot". Her LinkedIn post was later deleted — [NBC News, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/europe/mcdonalds-ai-generated-christmas-advert-social-media-backlash-rcna248590) (read 2026-10-15; [interview statement] via [press]). Another outlet gives "seven weeks" — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/the-most-terrible-ad-of-the-year-mcdonald-s-pulls-ai-ad) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]). This conflict is unresolved.
  - Estimate (mine): 10 people × 5 weeks × about 40 h is about 2,000 person-hours for 45 s, or **about 44 person-hours per finished second**.
- **Coca-Cola 2025:** the head counts conflict. Secret Level founder Jason Zada estimated 20 people against 50 without AI (The Hollywood Reporter, via [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/ai-generated-coca-cola-christmas-ad)). The behind-the-scenes video reportedly showed "over 100 workers" tweaking frames ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/ai-generated-coca-cola-christmas-ad)). Its voiceover said "five specialists" ([NBC Miami, 4 Nov 2025](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/national-international/coca-cola-ai-generated-holiday-ad-backlash/3716580/)). All read 2026-10-15; [secondary]/[press]. Secret Level: "Projects like this take an enormous amount of craft, coordination, and heart" ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/ai-generated-coca-cola-christmas-ad); [user post] via [secondary]).
- Coca-Cola's CMO said AI cut production from about a year to about a month — [retailboss.co](https://retailboss.co/coca-colas-ai-generated-holiday-campaign-and-the-backlash-that-went-viral) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary], [secondary], unverified).
- **Kalshi (June 2025):** one person, 2–3 days; workflow was script, then Gemini shot list and prompts, then Veo 3, then a human edit in CapCut or Premiere — [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/kalshis-nba-finals-ad-costs-just-2k-using-googles-ai-video-tool/) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]).
- **Netflix, El Eternauta (July 2025):** "Generative AI was also used, to create a shot of a collapsing building". Ted Sarandos said it was to "speed up production and save money" (Guardian, BBC, NYT, 18 Jul 2025) — [Wikipedia: The Eternaut (TV series)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eternaut_(TV_series)) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]). This is a single gen-AI shot inside a conventional VFX pipeline, i.e. hybrid use.
- **IAB (15 Jan 2026):** 83% of ad executives say their company uses AI in creative work (60% in 2024). 86% of buyers use or plan to use gen-AI for video ad creative (IAB 2025 Digital Video report). Cost efficiency is the top cited benefit at 64% — [IAB, "The AI Ad Gap Widens"](https://www.iab.com/insights/the-ai-ad-gap-widens/) (read 2026-10-15; [industry report]; US sample of 104 executives).

### Inferences
- Where the output must match a brand, a story and a taste bar, labour moves from shooting to generating, selecting, compositing and editing. It does not vanish. The McDonald's statement suggests it can exceed a shoot.
- The large gains come where exactness is low and chaos is the point (Kalshi), or where gen-AI does one discrete shot inside a normal pipeline (Netflix).

### Gaps
- WARC, Forrester, Gartner, a16z, McKinsey, WPP and Publicis statements were not reached (search budget exhausted). Studio production diaries with hour logs were not found.

## 7. Rights and licences

### Takeaway
Rights are unsettled in every market the client cares about, and none of it is fixed by better models. In the US, prompting alone does not make the user an author. Major training-data suits are unresolved, and opt-out schemes drew formal objections. Union rules price likeness use. Since 2026, India and the EU have binding labelling duties. Vendor indemnities exist but are narrow and conditional, and I could not confirm which ones cover video.

### Cited Findings — copyright in the output
- **US Copyright Office, Part 2: Copyrightability (29 Jan 2025):** "[G]iven current generally available technology, prompts alone do not provide sufficient human control to make users of an AI system the authors of the output". Copyright is available for human-authored perceptible material or for creative arrangement or modification of outputs — [IPWatchdog, 29 Jan 2025](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary] of [press] quoting [regulator]). Dates confirmed on the Office's page: Part 1 (digital replicas) 31 Jul 2024, Part 2 29 Jan 2025, Part 3 (training) pre-publication 9 May 2025, and no 2026 updates listed — [copyright.gov/ai](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/) (read 2026-10-15; [regulator]).
- **UK:** the copyright and AI consultation ran 17 Dec 2024 – 25 Feb 2025 and "addresses ... copyright protection for computer-generated works". No outcome has been published ("We are analysing your feedback"). DSIT published a report and impact assessment on 19 Mar 2026 — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence) (read 2026-10-15; [regulator]).
- **India:** not reached. This needs checking: the Copyright Act position on AI authorship and any DPIIT committee paper.

### Cited Findings — training data and lawsuits
- **Getty v Stability (UK High Court, 4 Nov 2025, [2025] EWHC 2863 (Ch)):** the secondary-infringement claim failed because the model weights did not store copies. Getty won only narrow trade-mark points on watermarks. Primary training claims were dropped. Permission to appeal was granted on 17 Dec 2025 — [AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/getty-images-v-stability-ai-uk-high-court-finds-no-secondary-copyright-infringement-in-landmark-genai-ruling/), [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/uk/stability-ai-defeats-getty-images-copyright-claims-in-first-of-its-kind-dispute-before-the-high-cour) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]; Bird & Bird returned 403 on fetch).
- **Disney & Universal v Midjourney** (filed 11 Jun 2025) and **Warner Bros. Discovery v Midjourney** (4 Sep 2025) were consolidated in November 2025. In July 2026 there was a discovery dispute over the studios' own AI use, and EVOX Productions filed a new suit (reported 31 Jul 2026). No merits ruling or settlement is reported — [Wikipedia: Midjourney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midjourney) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]).
- **Disney, Universal and WBD v MiniMax (Hailuo)**, filed 16 Sep 2025, C.D. Cal. It alleges Hailuo was built from the studios' IP and seeks an injunction, disgorgement and statutory damages. I found no 2026 status — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/disney-warner-bros-discovery-and-universal-file-joint-lawsuit-against-generative-ai-app-hailuo-163819825.html), [giga.law](https://giga.law/daily-news/2025/9/17/disney-universal-wbd-file-copyright-suit-against-chinas-minimax) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]). - **Disney cease-and-desist to Google (10 Dec 2025)** named Veo, Imagen, Nano Banana, Gemini and YouTube and alleged infringement "on a massive scale". Google removed the flagged YouTube videos. It is a letter, not a lawsuit — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/disney-google-cease-and-desist-ai-copyright-infringement/), [Variety AU](https://au.variety.com/2025/film/news/google-removes-ai-videos-disney-characters-cease-desist-31044) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]).
- **Sora 2 opt-out (from 30 Sep 2025):** copyrighted content was allowed by default with opt-out. On 3 Oct 2025 OpenAI promised "more granular control". On 6 Oct 2025 the MPA criticised the approach. On 17 Oct 2025 OpenAI paused MLK videos — [Wikipedia: Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora_(text-to-video_model)) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]). **CODA (Japan)**, on 28 Oct 2025, for members including Studio Ghibli, Bandai Namco, Square Enix and Aniplex, said "there is no system allowing one to avoid liability for infringement through subsequent objections" under Japanese law — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/sonys-aniplex-bandai-namco-and-other-japanese-publishers-demand-end-to-unauthorized-training-of-openais-sora2-through-coda/), [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-japanese-studios-openai-anime-manga-ai-training/) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]).
- **Disney–OpenAI deal** (11 Dec 2025): $1bn and a 3-year licence for 200+ characters on Sora 2. SAG-AFTRA, the WGA and The Animation Guild objected on 12 Dec 2025. The deal ended with Sora's shutdown in March 2026 — [Wikipedia: Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora_(text-to-video_model)), [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-is-shutting-down-its-sora-video-generation-app-211023358.html) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]/[press]).
- **Seedance 2.0 (Feb 2026):** after launch on 12 Feb 2026, Disney (13 Feb), Paramount (14–15 Feb), Netflix ("a high-speed piracy engine", 17 Feb), WBD and Sony sent letters, and the MPA sent a formal cease-and-desist (20 Feb). The CapCut rollout from 26 Mar 2026 excluded the US, blocks real faces in inputs and adds an invisible watermark ([Wikipedia: Seedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seedance); [secondary]). Seedance 2 and 2.5 are on sale via Runway's API today ([Runway API pricing](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/guides/pricing/); [primary doc]).

### Cited Findings — likeness, voice, unions

- Sora 2 estate backlash: families of Robin Williams, Kobe Bryant, Paul Walker and George Carlin asked OpenAI to restrict deepfakes on 8 Oct 2025 — [Wikipedia: Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora_(text-to-video_model)) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]).

### Cited Findings — disclosure and labelling
- **India: IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026**, notified 10 Feb 2026 and effective 20 Feb 2026. Synthetically generated information "must be prominently labelled and embedded with permanent metadata or provenance markers". Platforms may not allow markers to be removed. Significant social media intermediaries must "require users to declare whether content is SGI". Good-faith edits such as "formatting, colour adjustment, noise reduction, or compression" are excluded. Takedown on government or court order "within three hours" (was 36 h) — [Hogan Lovells, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary] law-firm summary). The notification number G.S.R. 120(E) comes from [search summary](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/story/govt-tightens-rules-on-ai-generated-content-mandates-labels-and-faster-takedowns-515505-2026-02-10). The Gazette text was not read.
- **EU AI Act Art. 50:** deepfake disclosure duties apply from 2 Aug 2026. The Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744) reportedly gave systems already on the market until 2 Dec 2026 for Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking only — [ICTRecht](https://www.ictrecht.nl/en/blog/heads-up-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-effect), [CSA research note, 29 Jul 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-20260729/) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]; Commission text not read; Ropes & Gray returned 403). The scope of the exception for artistic or fictional works needs checking against Art. 50(4).
- **YouTube:** "We require creators to disclose AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic", including "a realistic scene that didn't actually occur". Colour and lighting adjustments are exempt. Repeat non-disclosure risks removal or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program. YouTube may auto-label C2PA content — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]).
- **Meta:** "AI info" labels when it detects "industry standard AI image indicators or when people disclose". Political and social-issue ads must disclose digital creation or alteration. The page has an update dated 23 Oct 2025 — [Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]).
- **Veo** outputs are "watermarked using SynthID" — [Gemini API Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]).

### Inferences
- For a production company delivering to clients, the open legal questions are about **accountability**: who owns the output (US: possibly nobody, for unmodified generations), who is liable if a model reproduces a protected character or mark, whether the model in use is under a cease-and-desist, and whether the label and metadata duties are met. Model quality does not answer any of them.
- India's rules exclude colour adjustment, and YouTube exempts colour and lighting edits. Grading real footage is outside the labelling net, while realistic generated scenes are inside it. A hybrid "shoot the exact things, generate the rest" approach still carries labelling duties for the generated parts (inference; the exact thresholds need legal review).

- The UK ASA/CAP position on AI in ads, and Ofcom or Clearcast rules for UK broadcast ads, were not reached.
- The current Google indemnity list for Veo, Adobe's video and partner-model coverage, and Runway, Kling and ByteDance terms were not confirmed.
- Thaler v Perlmutter's final status could not be fetched (Wikipedia 404).

## 8. Public backlash and brand risk

### Takeaway
Backlash against fully AI ads is recurring and has made a brand pull an ad. The measured consumer–executive perception gap widened from 2024 to 2026. Better craft did not stop the backlash (Coca-Cola: "ten times better" craftsmanship in 2025, still criticised).

### Cited Findings
- **McDonald's Netherlands:** a 45-second AI Christmas ad by TBWA\Neboko and The Sweetshop went up on Saturday (6 Dec 2025) and was removed by 10 Dec 2025. McDonald's called it "an important learning" — [NBC News, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/europe/mcdonalds-ai-generated-christmas-advert-social-media-backlash-rcna248590) (read 2026-10-15; [press]), with the "important learning" quote from [search summary](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/diabolical-mcdonalds-pulls-ai-christmas-ad-after-backlash). Critics on X called it "unsettling", "creepy", "poorly edited" and "inauthentic" ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/europe/mcdonalds-ai-generated-christmas-advert-social-media-backlash-rcna248590); [user posts] via [press]).
- **Coca-Cola 2024:** its first AI holiday ads were "largely poorly received". **Coca-Cola 2025** (3 Nov 2025): comments included "Soulless", "immediately forgettable" and, on the behind-the-scenes video, "Jeez, this is dystopian". Coca-Cola's Pratik Thakar said they were guided by "human storytellers" and that last year's film "performed well with customers" — [NBC Miami, 4 Nov 2025](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/national-international/coca-cola-ai-generated-holiday-ad-backlash/3716580/) (read 2026-10-15; [press]). Thakar: "Last year people criticized the craftsmanship. But this year the craftsmanship is ten times better." (THR via [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/ai-generated-coca-cola-christmas-ad); [secondary]). A user post accusing Coca-Cola of a fake behind-the-scenes video drew about 68,000 likes in a day on 8 Nov 2025 (same source; [user post]).
- **Toys"R"Us** (2024, Sora): "online backlash ... described as creepy" — [Wikipedia: Toys "R" Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toys_%22R%22_Us) (read 2026-10-15; [secondary]).
- **IAB / Sonata (published 15 Jan 2026; fielded Oct 2025 – Jan 2026; 505 US Gen Z and Millennial consumers, 104 US executives):** 82% of executives think consumers feel positive about AI ads against 45% of consumers, a gap of 37 points (32 in 2024). Consumer negativity is up 12 points. 39% of Gen Z are negative (21% in 2024). Gen Z calls AI-using brands "inauthentic" (30%) and "unethical" (24%). 20% of consumers call such brands "manipulative" against 10% of executives. Fewer than half of AI-using advertisers always disclose. More than half of consumers want disclosure for fully AI ads and AI video — [IAB](https://www.iab.com/insights/the-ai-ad-gap-widens/) (read 2026-10-15; [industry report]).
- Contrary signal: a FreeWheel survey found the industry *over*-rates viewer hostility to AI creative — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/freewheel-finds-48-of-buyers-overrate-viewer-dislike-of-ai-ads/) (read 2026-10-15; [search summary]; not opened). This conflicts with IAB's direction.
- The Sora app's interest fell after launch: new downloads were down 32% in December 2025 against November (Appfigures) — [Engadget, 24 Mar 2026](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-is-shutting-down-its-sora-video-generation-app-211023358.html) (read 2026-10-15; [press]).

### Inferences
- The risk is concentrated in *visibly* AI work for brands built on warmth and craft (holiday ads), and among younger audiences. Disclosure and quality cannot be traded off: consumers want disclosure, and disclosure makes the AI visible.
- This gap is cultural and reputational, so model releases will not close it on their own. It may even widen as AI ads become more common (71% believe they have seen one, up from 54%; IAB).

### Gaps
- No India- or UK-specific consumer sentiment data on AI ads was found. The IAB sample is US only.
- Other 2025–26 backlash cases (e.g. Guess in Vogue, August 2025; fashion-house AI campaigns; the Primordial Soup / TIME "1776" series) were not reached, so they are unverified here.

## 9. Which gaps the big players will likely close soon, and which will stay open

### Takeaway
On the evidence of April 2025 to October 2026, vendors are closing **length, native audio, resolution, reference-based consistency, local editing and unit price** quickly. **Pixel-exact brand assets, numeric and deterministic control, physics and performance reliability, legal accountability, disclosure compliance, audience acceptance, labour for selection and fixing, and vendor stability** show either slow or no structural progress. These favour a hybrid, verified pipeline like the client's.

### Cited Findings (rate of progress, Apr 2025 – Oct 2026)
- Length: Veo 3 at 8 s (May 2025) → Veo 3.1 extend to 148 s at 720p (Oct 2025) → Seedance 2.0 at 15 s (Feb 2026) → Seedance 2.5 at 30 s, extendable (Jul 2026) → Kling 4.0 at native 30 s, 4K, 10-bit HDR (Oct 2026) — [Gemini API Veo docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) [primary doc]; [Wikipedia: Veo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veo_(text-to-video_model)), [Wikipedia: Seedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seedance) and [Wikipedia: Kling AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kling_AI) [secondary] (all read 2026-10-15).
- Audio: Veo 3 introduced synchronised audio (May 2025). Seedance 1.5 Pro (Dec 2025) and Seedance 2.0 (Feb 2026) followed, and Kling 3.0 has audio tiers — [Wikipedia: Veo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veo_(text-to-video_model)), [Wikipedia: Seedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seedance) [secondary]; [Runway API pricing](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/guides/pricing/) [primary doc] (read 2026-10-15).
- References: 3 images (Veo 3.1, Oct 2025) → 12 files (Seedance 2.0, Feb 2026) → 50 files (Seedance 2.5, Jul 2026; secondary) — sources as in §2.
- Editing: Aleph object add/remove/transform and new angles (Jul 2025); Veo Insert (Oct 2025) with Remove "soon"; Seedance 2.5 timestamp edits (Jul 2026) — sources as in §4.
- Price tiers: Google now sells Veo 3.1 Lite at $0.05–$0.08/s and Fast at $0.10–$0.12/s next to Standard at $0.40/s. Runway's API resells at least 10 model families — [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing), [Runway API pricing](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/guides/pricing/) (read 2026-10-15; [primary doc]). I could not read a dated 2025 price to compute the decline, so it needs checking.
- Persistent failures that vendors admit: object permanence, causal reasoning and success bias (Runway, Dec 2025) — [Runway](https://runway.com/research/introducing-runway-gen-4.5) [primary doc]. Physical understanding was "unrelated to visual realism" (Jan 2025) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09038) [paper]. Text was illegible or unstable in most of 10 systems (May 2025) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04946) [paper].
- Vendor instability: Sora 2 launched on 30 Sep 2025, its shutdown was announced on 24 Mar 2026, and the API ended on 24 Sep 2026 — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-is-shutting-down-its-sora-video-generation-app-211023358.html) [press], [Wikipedia: Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora_(text-to-video_model)) [secondary]. Seedance's global API was delayed after studio letters (Feb 2026) — [letsdatascience.com](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/bytedances-seedance-2.0-spooked-all-of-hollywood-in-72-hours) [secondary] (read 2026-10-15).
- Craft improved but backlash persisted: Coca-Cola's "ten times better" craftsmanship in 2025, and the IAB gap widening in 2026 — sources as in §8.

### Inferences (reasoned view; not vendor statements)
**Likely to close or narrow substantially within about 12–18 months, by the big players themselves:**
1. **Clip length** for ad and short-form use. It went from 8 s to 30 s native in about 17 months, with extension to minutes. Most ad shots are already within range.
2. **Native audio, resolution and frame quality.** Audio is now standard across leading families, and 4K and HDR are on offer.
3. **Character and object consistency from references.** Reference inputs grew more than tenfold in nine months, and this is the vendors' visible priority. Expect "good enough for most shots" consistency, not guaranteed identity. Object permanence remains an admitted failure.
4. **Local edits** (insert, remove, restyle, edit a time range). Shipping fast, though the "nothing else changes" guarantee is not documented.
5. **Unit price per generated second.** Tiering and resale competition are visible now. It is the *per kept second* cost (hit rate × price) that matters, and that depends on 1–4.

**Likely to stay open (structural, or outside what model upgrades fix):**
1. **Pixel-exact brand assets** (logo geometry, legal copy, packaging, a specified colour). The models are stochastic by design. Practitioners composite real assets even on the biggest budgets (Coca-Cola 2025), and there is no public colour-accuracy evidence at all. Even if text rendering improves, "usually right" is not acceptable for a logo or a regulated claim, and someone must verify every frame.
2. **Numeric, deterministic control** (lens, camera path, frame-accurate timing, a repeatable take). The docs I read offer words, keyframes and references, fixed duration buckets and 24 fps only, which is good for ideation and weak for specification.
3. **Physics and performance reliability.** Vendors still list causal-reasoning and success-bias failures. The most convincing action may be driven by real performers (the Seedance case, unresolved).
4. **Legal accountability**: authorship (USCO), unresolved training suits (Midjourney, MiniMax, the Getty appeal), cease-and-desists against current models (Veo, Seedance), likeness and union terms, and indemnities that are conditional and unclear for video. These are settled by courts, contracts and process, not by model releases.
5. **Disclosure compliance** (India's IT Rules since 20 Feb 2026, EU Art. 50 since 2 Aug 2026, YouTube and Meta). The duties grew over the period. An agent must label and attach metadata correctly, per market.
6. **Audience acceptance and taste.** The IAB gap widened while craft improved, and the McDonald's ad was pulled despite 10 people and 5 weeks of work.
7. **Human time for selection, fixing and editing.** Hit rates of about 4–5% (Kalshi) and tens of thousands of clips (Coca-Cola) mean selection labour scales with ambition. Better models raise the hit rate, but brand review and sign-off remain.
8. **Vendor dependency.** Sora's shutdown six months after launch, and Seedance's delayed global API, show that a single-model agent can lose its engine or face access restrictions (e.g. Veo's adults-only person generation in the UK and EU).

**Implication for the client's thesis (inference):** the evidence supports "generation alone can't be trusted for anything that must be exact". It also suggests where an agent adds value the vendors are not building: deterministic handling of exact elements (real product, logo, text and colour composited and verified), measurable QA against a spec, hit-rate reduction through planning, multi-vendor routing, and rights, labelling and audit trails per market (India, UK, EU).

### Gaps
- No quantitative benchmark tracking hit rates or "usable output" over time was reached, so the rate of improvement in *usable* output (as opposed to specs) is unmeasured here.
- Vendors' public roadmaps (Google, Runway, Kling, ByteDance) for exact text, logo or colour control were not reached.
- Maker statements from the Toys"R"Us and Puma projects, AI film-festival makers' hit rates, and the WARC, Forrester, Gartner, a16z and McKinsey reports were not reached (shared search budget exhausted, Reddit unreachable). A follow-up pass could fill these.
