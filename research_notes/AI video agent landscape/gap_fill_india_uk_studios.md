## 1. India: rules for AI-generated content (IT Rules, ASCI, copyright and training, personality and voice rights)

### Takeaway
India now has two layers of rules. The binding IT Rules amendment (in force since 20 Feb 2026) puts labelling and metadata duties on **platforms and AI-tool providers**, not directly on a production house that delivers a file. ASCI's self-regulatory guidelines (published 29 Sep 2026, in force about 29 Dec 2026) bind **advertisers**. They ask for a label only when synthetic content materially affects a buying decision, and they ban fake endorsements, exaggerated results and unconsented likenesses even if labelled. Courts grant fast, often ex parte, injunctions against AI voice and likeness clones. On copyright, training is provisionally fair dealing (ANI v OpenAI, an interim order now under appeal), and policy leans towards a mandatory paid licence. Who owns AI output is unresolved.

### Cited Findings
**IT Rules amendment 2026 (law; read through a law firm's note with annexure text)**
- Notified 10 Feb 2026, in force 20 Feb 2026 — [law-firm note, Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas, Feb 2026](https://www.amsshardul.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Legislative-Update-IT-Rules-Amendment.pdf). Notification G.S.R. 120(E) — [DSCI](https://www.dsci.in/resource/content/it-amendment-rules-2026), [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/government-notifies-information-technology-amendment-rules-2026/) (search summary). The Gazette PDF itself was not read.
- **Definition:** "synthetically generated information" (SGI) is AV content "artificially or algorithmically created, generated, modified, or altered" so that it appears real and depicts a person or event as indistinguishable from reality. **Exempt when done in good faith:** routine editing, formatting or technical correction; transcription, translation, subtitling, **dubbing**, captions; documents and training material that do not purport to show real people or events — [AMS note](https://www.amsshardul.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Legislative-Update-IT-Rules-Amendment.pdf).
- **Who it binds** — [AMS note](https://www.amsshardul.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Legislative-Update-IT-Rules-Amendment.pdf):
  - **All intermediaries:** remind users every three months, act on their own initiative against unlawful SGI, and deploy automated tools to prevent unlawful SGI (including content that "falsely depicts or misrepresents a natural person or real-world event").
  - **Intermediaries offering a tool that creates SGI** (i.e. AI generators): visual content must carry "a prominently visible disclosure"; audio needs "a prominently prefixed audio disclosure"; "permanent metadata or equivalent provenance mechanisms, to the extent technically feasible, including a unique identifier" naming the tool used.
  - **Significant social media intermediaries (YouTube, Meta and similar):** before publication, require users to **declare** whether content is SGI, verify the declaration with technical measures, then label it.
  - **Users (creators):** penalties under the IT Act and other laws for unlawful SGI.
- **Takedown times:** lawful direction 36 h → **3 h**; intimate-image complaints 24 h → 2 h; grievances 15 → 7 days — [AMS note](https://www.amsshardul.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Legislative-Update-IT-Rules-Amendment.pdf).
- **Advertisers** are not named in the IT Rules. "The IT Rules require intermediaries to label all synthetically generated content, whereas the Guidelines apply to advertisers and require labelling only where the content materially influences consumer decisions" — [law-firm note, Khaitan & Co, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-10/Ergo_ASCI%20Guidelines%20on%20SGC%20Labelling_12%2010%202026.pdf).

**ASCI Guidelines for Responsible Labelling of Synthetically Generated Content in Advertising (guideline text read)**
- **Draft** issued May 2026 (press release dated 12 May 2026; comments due 13 Jun 2026) with the labels "Audio/Video created using AI" or "Audio/Video enhanced using AI" — [Manifest Media](https://www.manifest-media.in/advertising/120526/asci-drafts-guidelines-for-labelling-of-ai-generated-content-in-ads.html), [DSCI brief](https://www.dsci.in/files/content/knowledge-centre/2026/brief-asci-guidelines.pdf) (search summary; the DSCI brief misdates the draft to 2025). The draft's high/medium/low risk tiers were dropped in the final text — [Khaitan, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-10/Ergo_ASCI%20Guidelines%20on%20SGC%20Labelling_12%2010%202026.pdf).
- **Final text** signed by the Chairman of ASCI's Board of Governors on **17 Sep 2026**; it comes into effect "on the expiration of 3 months from the date of their publication" — [guideline text (PDF hosted by Fox Mandal)](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf). Released **29 Sep 2026**, so effective **29 Dec 2026** — [Khaitan](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-10/Ergo_ASCI%20Guidelines%20on%20SGC%20Labelling_12%2010%202026.pdf). exchange4media (30 Sep 2026) gave 1 Jan 2027 — [e4m](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/asci-issues-guidelines-on-responsible-labelling-of-ai-generated-ad-content-158710.html) (search summary; 403 on fetch). **Conflict:** the two dates differ.
- **Scope:** "applies to all advertisers deploying SGC"; breach is a breach of the ASCI Code, which can lead to "recommendations for modification or withdrawal" — [Khaitan](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-10/Ergo_ASCI%20Guidelines%20on%20SGC%20Labelling_12%2010%202026.pdf).
- **Three tests** for whether a label is needed: is SGC "central to an ad's persuasive messaging or materially influences purchase decisions"; does it mislead about claims or performance if unlabelled; would no disclosure create a false impression — [guideline text](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf).
- **Prohibited even with a label:** fabricated endorsements or testimonials; exaggerated results or features (a weight-loss before/after example); presenting non-existent locations as real; "Using unauthorised copyrighted work, deepfakes or a person's likeness without consent" — [guideline text](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf).
- **Label required:** synthetic influencers or ambassadors; a real person's likeness or voice replicated **even with consent** for personalised messaging; fabricated events or settings that affect understanding of product performance; a product that does not yet exist ("a 3D model of an unbuilt housing complex"); sound effects central to the product (a headset's audio); paid AI recommendations ("Sponsored by [Brand]") — [guideline text](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf).
- **No label needed:** "Routine editing, colour correction, light adjustment, noise reduction, standard blemish removal and minor lighting tweaks"; decorative "backgrounds, abstract skylines, ambient music, jingles, or background sound effects" unrelated to the product's capabilities; "Obvious, unrealistic effects" (a fairy with magic dust; a car turning into a spaceship); copy and admin uses; accessibility (subtitles, translations). Labels may be the platform's own or e.g. "Audio/Video created using AI". No size or duration rule is set beyond ASCI's existing disclaimer guidelines — [guideline text](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf).

### Inferences
- A production house that delivers finished files to a brand is probably not an "intermediary". If Das later opens its agent to outside users as a generation tool, it may become one "offering a computer resource" for SGI, which brings visible-label, audio-prefix and permanent-metadata duties. This needs Indian legal advice before any public launch.
- Uploads will be labelled anyway: YouTube, Meta and similar platforms must ask the uploader to declare SGI. The agent should prepare the declaration and embed C2PA-style metadata, so the brand or agency uploading is not caught out.
- ASCI's "no label" list (colour correction, light adjustment, decorative backgrounds, ambient music, obvious fantasy) fits Das's "real exact parts, generated surface" method well. What does need a label is a **realistic generated product demonstration**, an **unreleased product**, or a **voice or likeness replica** (even with consent). An exaggerated product depiction is **banned** outright. A product built exactly from the client's 3D or CAD file is a compliance asset, because it cannot overstate the product.
- Indian courts readily grant ex parte, dynamic, John Doe injunctions over voice and likeness. Any sound-alike or look-alike of a known Indian performer, Bengali stars included, is high risk without written consent. The agent should refuse celebrity likeness or voice by default and keep a consent log.

## 2. UK: ASA/CAP on AI in ads, the copyright and AI outcome, Getty v Stability appeal

### Takeaway
The UK has **no AI-specific ad-labelling rule**. The CAP Code applies "in full", with misleading endorsements, harm and offence the live risks, and the advertiser stays liable for AI output. On copyright, the government's March 2026 report dropped the opt-out TDM exception as its preferred option but changed no law. It intends to remove protection for purely computer-generated works eventually, and it pushed transparency, labelling and a possible personality right into further work. Getty's appeal on secondary infringement had not been heard as of 15 Oct 2026, as far as I could find.

### Cited Findings
**ASA/CAP**
- CAP News, **11 Jun 2026**, "AI and Deepfakes: Four Things Advertisers Need to Know Before They Hit 'Run'" [regulator]: the Code is media-neutral. "If an AI‑generated celebrity 'endorsement' looks real enough to mislead consumers, it's likely breaching the Code". "the advertiser remains responsible for any output used in their ads". AI bias is a compliance issue. Automated campaigns do not shift responsibility. The page does **not** address disclosing AI use — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/ai-and-deepfakes-four-things-advertisers-need-to-know-before-they-hit-run.html).
- Rulings cited: **Polyverse Inc** (AI Mirror ad shown in a game, Sep 2023; socially irresponsible, offensive). **Cosmos Oyun** (AI video app ad, Apr 2025, a "kiss your celebrity crush" deepfake of Sabrina Carpenter; harmful, offensive, a gender stereotype). **Stripe & Stare** (ad built with Google's automated tools; advertiser "primarily responsible") — [Lewis Silkin, 18 Jun 2026](https://lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/06/18/asa-warns-on-ai-generated-content-and-deepfakes-102n4o1) [law-firm note].
- On disclosure, CAP's position is that there is no blanket duty. It suggests two questions: "Is the audience likely to be misled if the use of AI is not disclosed?", and whether a disclosure clarifies or contradicts the message — [RPC, summer 2025](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/summer-2025/cap-issues-guidance-on-use-of-ai-in-ads/), [Mills & Reeve](https://www.mills-reeve.com/publications/disclosure-of-ai-in-advertising/) (search summary; [law-firm note]).

**Copyright and AI outcome**
- The GOV.UK consultation page still reads "We are analysing your feedback". No formal "outcome" document is posted — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence) [regulator].
- **Report and impact assessment, 18 Mar 2026** (DSIT, DCMS and IPO; required by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025): "no immediate reform to UK copyright law". The opt-out TDM exception is "no longer the Government's preferred option", but it is not formally abandoned. Transparency is left to "work with industry to explore good practice". Protection for computer-generated works (s.9(3) CDPA) "will ultimately be removed", with the decision deferred; AI-assisted works that meet the originality test stay protected. Reed Smith reads the impact assessment as implying that unlicensed commercial training is unlikely to be lawful under current UK law ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/uk-copyright-and-ai-report-the-opt-out-is-dead-but-what-comes-next/)).
- The digital-replica / personality-right consultation "has not yet opened" as of mid-2026 — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/ai-deepfakes-and-image-rights-does-english-law-protect-your-face-voice-and-likeness/1846956), [LawCareers, 2 Jun 2026](https://www.lawcareers.net/Explore/CommercialQuestion/RPC-Deepfakes-and-digital-replicas-the-UKs-legal-patchwork) (search summary). **Getty v Stability appeal**

### Inferences
- In the UK, the compliance work is the familiar advertising test (misleading, harmful, offensive, fake endorsement), not labelling. India is now more prescriptive (ASCI tiers, IT Rules metadata). One agent needs a **per-market policy table**: India from 29 Dec 2026, the UK under the CAP test, and the EU under Art. 50 (see `gaps_evidence.md`).
- If computer-generated-works protection is removed, purely generated UK footage may carry **no copyright**. Client contracts should cover ownership of the human-made parts (script, edit, 3D assets, composited real product), because those are the parts that remain protected.

### Inferences
- For pricing comparisons (my estimate, not reported): a mid-tier Indian brand film appears to sit around ₹15–60 lakh to produce, and a mid-tier UK spot around £30,000–100,000. Vendors claim AI versions at about 15–30% of those numbers. Treat these as **sales-page bands**; Das's own past budgets are a better baseline.
- AI's saving shows up mostly in **versioning, regional language variants and social cut-downs** (Tata Gluco+'s regional variants in a week, Bata's always-on assets), less in the hero film.
