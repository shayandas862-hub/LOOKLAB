## 1. Who buys AI video tools and AI-made video, and what they pay

### Takeaway
The largest verified spend today comes from enterprises buying avatar and presenter video for training, sales enablement and localisation (Synthesia, HeyGen), and from creators, marketers and SMBs buying credit-based generation (Higgsfield, Kling, Runway, invideo). Film, TV and advertising buyers are targeted openly but disclose little. Tools cost **$10–$300 a month** per seat on public plans, and API generation costs **about $0.03–$0.70 per second** of output. The one well-documented AI ad (Kalshi, June 2025) cost about **$2,000**, against production-company quotes of "six or seven figures". That case is an outlier, and a lot of the publicity around it came from the novelty.

**AI-made ads and films compared with traditional production (published cases)**
- **Kalshi, NBA Finals ad (aired June 2025).** Made by AI filmmaker PJ Accetturo, "generated entirely using Veo 3", with Gemini and ChatGPT used for the shot list and prompts. Kalshi's spokesperson said it cost "roughly $2,000 to make" and went from idea to live in three days; Accetturo said "TWO DAYS". It took **300–400 generations to get 15 usable clips**. Production companies had quoted Kalshi in the "six or seven figure range". Accetturo said AI work pays him better than live-action jobs. — [Yahoo Entertainment, updated 13 Jun 2025 (secondary)](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/articles/chaotic-kalshi-ad-during-nba-173937071.html); reception described as "chaotic" by Business Insider — [Wikipedia: Kalshi (secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalshi)
- **Dashverse (Bengaluru), *Raftaar*.** A 90-minute AI microdrama. The company claims production time was halved and costs cut by **75%** against traditional methods, with "a single creator" going from script to final cut "in days". — [afaqs, 9 Sep 2025 (secondary; company claim)](https://www.afaqs.com/news/media/dashverse-creates-indias-first-completely-ai-generated-microdrama-10065088)
- **Netflix, *El Eternauta*.** Gen AI made a building-collapse shot. Ted Sarandos said it was used to speed up production and save money. — [Wikipedia citing BBC / Guardian / NYT, 18 Jul 2025 (secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eternaut_(TV_series))
- **Amazon, *House of David* (The Wonder Project).** Season 1 had **72 AI shots**. Season 2 had **350–400**, more than 100 of them for LED virtual-production backgrounds. Tools were Midjourney, Magnific, Topaz, Runway and Kling, plus Unreal, Nuke, Photoshop and After Effects. No cost figures were published. — [Wikipedia citing Variety 20 Mar 2025 and Wired 10 Nov 2025 (secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_David_(TV_series))

### Inferences
- **The compute cost of a 30-second film is small compared with what clients pay.** At Veo 3.1 Standard rates ($0.40/s with audio), 30 s of final footage costs $12 if every take works. Kalshi's yield was about 4–5% (15 usable clips from 300–400 generations). At that yield, generation for a 30-s spot is roughly **$300–$1,300** at standard rates, or a quarter of that on Fast/Lite tiers. This is my arithmetic from the cited prices and yield, not a published figure. Labour (prompting, selecting, editing, sound, approvals) is the main cost, which matches Kalshi's total of about $2,000.
- The buyers who pay at scale and repeatedly are enterprise L&D, comms and localisation teams (Synthesia, HeyGen) and high-volume social and performance marketers (Higgsfield, Mirage, invideo). A "warm, premium 30-second launch film" serves brand and agency buyers. That segment has fewer public numbers and judges quality much more harshly.
- invideo (India-founded) prices in USD only and resells third-party models (Seedance). The consumer one-prompt-to-video tier is already crowded and is priced from about $10–$60 a month.

### Gaps
- Kling and Higgsfield plan prices could not be read (page not fetched, or rendered empty). Check them directly.
- No published price list was found for an AI-made ad or film sold by a production company in India or the UK. Traditional TVC rates in India and the UK could not be researched after the search budget ran out. Both are needed for a proper comparison.
- No API-spend totals by buyer type were found.
- HeyGen's and Synthesia's enterprise contract sizes are not published.

## 2. Market size: named sources, dates and how the estimates were made

### Takeaway
Analyst estimates for an "AI video generator" market sit below **$1B for 2025–2026**: Grand View Research $788.5M (2025), Fortune Business Insights $946.4M (2026). Broader definitions reach $3.7B (Meticulous Research, 2026). These look too low. A handful of vendors' own run rates already add up to more than $1.5B a year: Kling about $500M (Mar 2026), Higgsfield $700M (Aug 2026), HeyGen more than $200M (Jun 2026), Synthesia more than $100M (Apr 2025). Treat all analyst figures as rough estimates whose methods are not public.

### Inferences
- Converted at roughly 7.2 RMB per USD (my conversion), Kling's Q2 revenue of more than RMB850M is about **$118M+ a quarter**. That one product alone is close to the whole of Grand View's or Fortune BI's 2025–2026 "market". The analyst reports probably use narrow or out-of-date definitions. For a pitch or plan, company-reported revenue is the sounder evidence, with the caveat that ARR and run-rate figures are not audited.
- Most visible revenue sits with platform owners: model labs and aggregators. No public figure separates "AI-made video sold as a service", which is the money a production company would earn, from tool spend.

### Gaps

The headline Disney–OpenAI deal fell away when **OpenAI shut Sora** (app 26 Apr 2026, API 24 Sep 2026). That is a clear warning about building on a single vendor's model.

## 4. What production companies, studios and agencies are doing with AI video now (global and India)

### Takeaway
Big buyers use AI for **parts** of the job: pre-vis, storyboards, single VFX shots, set extensions, LED backgrounds, marketing assets and localisation. The fully AI-made items are short ads (Kalshi), microdramas (Dashverse) and mythological or period IP where there are no actors to protect (Galleri5's *Mahabharat* TV series and the delayed *Chiranjeevi Hanuman* feature). In India, **Collective Artists Network / Galleri5** is the most advanced: Star Plus and JioHotstar broadcast, a Star Studio18 feature, and a 200-model "AI Studio". There is strong pushback from established filmmakers. Agencies (WPP) are building in-house AI-plus-virtual-production hubs, including in London.

## 5. Business models for a small production company in AI video today

### Takeaway
Three models have evidence behind them. (1) **Service studio** that sells finished AI or hybrid ads and content at well below traditional quotes but far above compute cost (Kalshi/Accetturo; Galleri5 doing TV and branded content). (2) **Platform/SaaS** with subscriptions plus credits. This is where most of the money is, but it is dominated by heavily funded companies (Higgsfield, HeyGen, Synthesia, invideo, Mirage). (3) **Hybrid tool-plus-owned-IP or distribution** (Dashverse with its own app, Runway–Lionsgate co-developed IP, Moonvalley owning Asteria, Galleri5 with its own slate). For a small Kolkata team, there is little direct evidence that an independent SaaS can win against $4–5B competitors. The evidence that a lean AI-enabled service business can earn well per job is anecdotal but real.

### Cited Findings
- Service model: one AI filmmaker delivered a national TV ad for about $2,000 in 2–3 days, against production quotes in the "six or seven figure range". He says AI work pays better than his live-action jobs. — [Yahoo, 13 Jun 2025 (secondary)](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/articles/chaotic-kalshi-ad-during-nba-173937071.html)
- Platform model at scale: Higgsfield ($700M annualised revenue, $5.4B valuation) sells to "creators, marketers, and studios". — [Reuters via The Star (secondary)](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/08/17/higgsfield039s-valuation-soars-fourfold-to-54-billion-in-six-months-on-ai-content-demand). HeyGen is above $200M ARR. — [HeyGen (primary)](https://www.heygen.com/blog/heygen-surpasses-200m-arr). Mirage is building "assembly intelligence", which is automatic video assembly from many inputs. — [Trending Topics (search summary)](https://trendingtopics.eu/mirage-raises-75m-to-push-ai-video-app-captions-into-asian-markets/)
- Aggregator and wrapper model: invideo and Higgsfield resell third-party models (Seedance, Kling, Veo) behind their own credits. — [invideo pricing (primary)](https://invideo.io/pricing). Figma bought Weavy, a multi-model node canvas. — [SiliconANGLE (search summary)](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/30/figma-acquires-ai-design-startup-weavy-reported-200m/)
- Hybrid with owned distribution: Dashverse built Frameo.AI and makes money on its own DashReels app ($2M in Aug 2025). — [afaqs (secondary)](https://www.afaqs.com/news/media/dashverse-creates-indias-first-completely-ai-generated-microdrama-10065088)
- Hybrid talent-agency studio: Collective Artists Network runs Galleri5 as its tech arm, supplies broadcasters (Star Plus, JioHotstar) and co-produces its own slate (Historyverse). — [MediaNews4U (secondary)](https://www.medianews4u.com/collective-artists-network-galleri5-launch-indias-first-cinema-native-production-operating-system-at-the-india-ai-impact-summit/)
- Model lab plus studio plus IP: Runway and Lionsgate co-develop IP with Lionsgate holding equity in Runway; Moonvalley owns Asteria. — [Runway (primary)](https://runway.com/news/runway-and-lionsgate-expand-partnership); [Wikipedia: Natasha Lyonne (secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natasha_Lyonne)
- Agencies are bringing production in-house with AI and virtual production (WPP's London hub; WPP Production's ~10,000 craft staff), which competes with independent production houses for brand work. — [WPP (primary)](https://www.wpp.com/en/news/2026/09/wpp-launches-flagship-production-hub-in-east-london). Morgan Stanley expects gen AI to drive agency consolidation. — [Morgan Stanley (search summary)](https://www.morganstanley.com/ideas/thoughts-on-the-market-metayer-generative-ai-ad-agency)
- Platform risk: Sora's API and app were discontinued (API on 24 Sep 2026). — [OpenAI developer docs (primary)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/sora-2). Price tiers and credits change often, and Runway and invideo credits mostly do not roll over. — [Runway pricing (primary)](https://runway.com/pricing); [invideo pricing (primary)](https://invideo.io/pricing)

### Inferences
- For Das Production, the evidence favours a **hybrid sequence**. First, use the agent internally to run an AI-first service studio for brands in India and the UK. There, price is set by what the client saves against a traditional shoot, not by compute cost (generation for a 30-s film is roughly $300–$1,300 at current API prices, per §1). Second, productise only the parts that prove repeatable. A direct-to-consumer "one prompt to finished video" SaaS would compete with Higgsfield, invideo, HeyGen and Mirage, which already have tens of millions of users and $75–400M rounds.
- What sets a small studio apart is not the model, since every competitor rents the same Veo, Kling and Seedance. It is control and taste: consistent characters and products, brand-safe sound and music, all aspect ratios delivered, and human sign-off. This is the "orchestration" Galleri5 also sells.
- UK angle: Synthesia's 70% FTSE 100 reach shows UK enterprises buying AI video for comms and training. WPP's London hub shows agencies keeping premium production in-house. A small outside studio is more likely to win mid-market brands and agencies that need overflow capacity than to win FTSE enterprise L&D.

### Gaps
- No public P&L, rates or margins were found for small AI-video service studios in India or the UK. "Who is making money" at small scale rests on single anecdotes (Accetturo/Kalshi).
- Not checked: how agencies price AI-made work to clients (day rates, per-asset or retainer), freelance AI-director rates on marketplaces, and any India-specific rate cards. These need follow-up searches when budget allows.
